PHYSICS ILLUSTRATION
Kinetic energy = one half × mass × speed squared. Impact may transfer
energy into structural deformation, fragmentation, ground displacement,
heat and sound. Fuel burning and a high-explosive detonation are different
processes; an observed fireball does not by itself establish a warhead.
Ground composition, moisture, pavement, breakup and impact geometry also
affect the final depression. No reverse calculation of object identity,
missile/drone probability, TNT yield or measured crater geometry is made.

The main film uses a separate illustrative terminal section at 20–27
seconds, contact near 26.5 seconds, and an aerial context view at 27–30
seconds, followed by inspection controls. These are presentation timings,
not the actual duration or acceleration history of the terminal descent.
The terminal aircraft, pieces, smoke, fire and ground scar are original
renderer elements, not extracted wreckage geometry or surveyed damage.
The map uses true-north geographic coordinates; the nose follows the
illustrated direction of travel, not a permanently north-facing attitude.

WEATHER AND NARRATION LIMITS
Archived weather and the crew's reported icing/turbulence provide context.
Radar alone does not recreate exact cockpit conditions, ice accumulation,
three-dimensional cloud boundaries or the cause of loss of control.
Terminal-scene haze and lighting are illustrative rendering treatments.

The revised narrator uses device speech synthesis with improved voice
selection and pacing. Its quality depends on installed/browser-provided
voices. Recorded-natural-voice generation was blocked by the provider's
credit limit; no MP3 was generated. The test browser exposed no English
voice, so audible voice quality was not verified there. Generated narration
is clearly distinct from the linked third-party ATC excerpt.
